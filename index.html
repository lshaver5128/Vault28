⚡ The Founder's Journey

The Story Behind Vault 28

From marketplace hustling to uncovering vintage 1st Edition treasures, to going all-in on Pokémon.

How It All Started

“Vault 28 started from a genuine passion for the hunt. What began as browsing local sports card collections on Facebook Marketplace turned into an unexpected discovery of vintage 1st Edition Pokémon holos — and that moment changed everything.”

📦 1. Starting Off with Sports Cards on Marketplace

I originally began by hunting sports card collections and lots across Facebook Marketplace. I loved digging through binders, learning card conditions, analyzing comps, and offering straightforward, fair cash buyouts to folks looking to clean off their shelves.

✨ 2. The Breakthrough: Vintage 1st Edition Holos

Then came the collection that changed everything. I took a chance on an unassuming deal and got incredibly lucky: it was packed to the brim with vintage 1st Edition Pokémon cards and iconic WOTC holos. Holding those childhood grails reignited an obsession. The nostalgic energy, community passion, and timeless artwork in Pokémon completely blew sports cards out of the water.

🎪 3. Going All-In & Hitting the Road Vending

From that turning point, I made the decision to pivot 100% into Pokémon. I launched Vault 28 Trading Co., invested in a full live vending booth setup, and hit the road at card conventions and shows. Today, we're proud to buy raw collections on the spot for cash, trade grails from our inventory, and connect directly with fellow collectors.

🛠️ Connect Google Firebase

Step 1: Go to Firebase Console Open console.firebase.google.com and sign in with your Google account.
Step 2: Create a Project Click Add Project, name it (e.g. Vault28), disable Google Analytics (recommended for fast setup), and click Create.
Step 3: Register a Web App In your project overview, click the Web icon (</>) to register an app. Name it Vault28Web and click register.
Step 4: Copy Config Keys Firebase will show you a configuration script. Copy only the firebaseConfig object. It looks like this: 
const firebaseConfig = {
  apiKey: "AIzaSy...",
  authDomain: "vault28.firebaseapp.com",
  projectId: "vault28",
  storageBucket: "vault28.appspot.com",
  messagingSenderId: "123456...",
  appId: "1:123456..."
};
Step 5: Paste in app.js Open app.js in VS Code and paste your config into the FIREBASE_CONFIG variable at the very top of the file. Save and push the change to your GitHub repo!
